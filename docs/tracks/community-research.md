# Track 3: Community Research

Who is RideScore for? Cyclists, scooter riders, policy makers, advocacy groups? In this community and user research track, we explore who we think RideScore DC is built for and what we need to ask users to understand the map: can they trust it, and can they use it to make decisions?

You don't need to code or be a bike-safety expert. Your fresh perspective and diversity of thought is the point. Teams work from a kit of persona cards, testing guides and shared templates to start researching.

## Open problems

- Test the map with users to find what's confusing
- Compare street scores with rider (or pedestrian) experience
- Review how accessible the map is, and how well it serves riders across all of DC's wards
- Write accessible, plain-language explanations of how the score works and what it can't tell you
- Compare RideScore DC with peer bike-safety tools

## Before you come

No software installation required.
